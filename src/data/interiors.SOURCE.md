# Sources for `interiors.json`

Retrieved on 2026-10-12. These are school diagrams, not seismic models. Each boundary is one radius. Local variation (a thicker crust under continents, a fuzzy core) is omitted. `displayFraction` is the drawing, not the true radius: a shell thinner than 8% of the mesh would be a few pixels at the focus distance, so thin shells are thickened. The kilometers in the card are the `outerRadiusKm` values.

`outerRadiusKm` of the outer layer is the volumetric mean radius from `bodies.json`.

## Sun

NASA Marshall Solar Physics, "The Solar Interior": the core is the inner 25%, the radiative zone reaches 70% of the radius, and the convection zone is the rest.

<https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml>

## Mercury

NASA describes a metal core about 85% of the radius and a thin rocky shell. The diagram uses a 35 km crust (a MESSENGER-era round figure) and a core radius of 0.85 × 2439.7 km. The split of the rocky shell into crust and mantle is a teaching choice.

<https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/>

## Venus

No mission has measured these boundaries. The diagram uses a 50 km crust and a core radius of 3000 km, an Earth-like estimate. Both numbers are rough.

<https://science.nasa.gov/venus/>

## Earth

USGS "Inside the Earth": the crust is about 5–70 km, the mantle ends near 2890 km depth, and the inner core begins near 5150 km depth. With a volumetric radius of 6371 km that is an outer-core radius of 3481 km and an inner-core radius of 1221 km. The diagram draws the crust as 35 km, a single thickness in the middle of that range.

<https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/inside.html>

## Moon

A teaching split: crust 40 km (GRAIL puts the average near that), mantle, and a core of 350 km. The core radius is a round figure inside the usual 200–400 km range, not a single measurement.

<https://science.nasa.gov/moon/>

## Mars

NASA JPL on InSight: the core radius is about 1830 km. The crust is drawn as 50 km, inside the range InSight allowed, not a single global thickness.

<https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-insight-reveals-the-deep-interior-of-mars>

## Jupiter and Saturn

Textbook gas-giant cuts. Jupiter: metallic hydrogen from 0.8 of the 1-bar radius, a core at 0.2. Saturn: metallic hydrogen from 0.65, a core at 0.25. Juno and Cassini show that the cores are dilute and the boundaries are not sharp. The card says so. There is no hard surface; the outer layer is the atmosphere and the deep gas.

- <https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/>
- <https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/>

## Uranus and Neptune

Textbook ice-giant cuts: an atmosphere down to 0.8 of the radius, a hot "ice" mantle of water and gases, and a rocky core at 0.2. These fractions are models. The mantle is not solid ice.

- <https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/>
- <https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/>

## What is left out

The Galilean moons (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto) have no entry. Europa's ocean is a later diagram. Colors are chosen so the layers read on a black background. They are not measured.
